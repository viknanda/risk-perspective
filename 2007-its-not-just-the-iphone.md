# It's Not Just the iPhone!

Ten days to the official launch of the iPhone - perhaps one of the most anticipated introductions of technology ever! Steve Jobs may wince at the following comparison, but it is one of the few that comes to mind when I think of “really hot” technology launches - Windows 95. (Did I just hear him say, “Ok, this is kind of weird.”) Recently, we have experienced the buzz around video game consoles such as the PlayStation 3, Xbox 360 and the Wii. However, I will argue that an iPod, mobile phone and Internet device packed in one has the potential to reach many more people than a video game console (maybe the Wii may change that). Similarly, Windows 95 was a major overhaul of the Microsoft Windows operating system that introduced features such as “Plug n Play” and a brand new graphical user interface (GUI) - it definitely reached consumers the world over and changed the personal computer (PC) experience for good.

While only time will tell whether or not the iPhone will change the 21st century consumer, I want to discuss the impact of its premature announcement and the run-up to the actual launch. I have to admit that the day the iPhone was announced, I was glued to the screen, squealing with child-like delight and fascination. I was confined to a three-walled cube in a skyscraper in Lower Manhattan, but all that mattered at that moment were the packets of data crossing the wire, updating a live text and graphics feed of the event. A fellow co-worker in the neighboring aisle screamed with joy at almost every new feature that Jobs announced - it was cool to feel the same way. Just about every popular and not-so-popular media site has covered the iPhone because not covering it means your franchise is un-cool, un-Web 2.0 and un-GenY. People of varying ages have been discussing the iPhone and its features, whether it will be worth the $499 or $599 and a 2-year contract with the new AT&T. The new AT&T is living up to its newness by not revealing any information on potential data plans for the iPhone. Rumor has it that employees of AT&T have their vacation frozen in order to prepare for the pre-launch, launch and post-launch of the iPhone.

Talking about impact, there is probably no greater tangible measure than Apple's stock price - up by approximately 47% since the iPhone announcement (6 months). While the relatively short-term gain may be attributed to the iPhone, I think that it is unjust and short-sighted to credit only the iPhone for Apple's performance. What about the fact that Apple's stock has doubled in the last 11 months, before any introduction of the iPhone? Or how about the 2.5 billion songs that Apple's iTunes store has sold till now? And what about the gadget that helped re-launch Apple as a technology leader, the iPod, of which there are 100 million units in consumers' hands?

The iPhone is just one example of the output from a successful technology company, which truly seems to understand the current information revolution. The common characteristic among Apple's products and services is providing users a simplified means of playing with technology. This is a consequence of coupling Apple's traditional edge in design as well as an advanced comprehension of user abilities and desires with respect to technology. I speculate that somewhere within Apple, there is strategy genius, which seems to execute alongside the human senses. Check this - iPod (hearing), AppleTV (vision + hearing) and iPhone (touch + vision + hearing). I feel awe-struck (and a little cheated) to realize that all those gadgets were created with the ultimate goal of creating a link with one of my human senses and not just a non-living package of semiconductors and other electronic circuitry!

The motivation for my commentary is a result of observing the type of media coverage and attention that the iPhone has received. For some odd reason, we want to dub it as the be-all or end-all for Apple and for consumers alike. While that reaction on the part of society does not surprise me, it certainly has led me to take a very objective approach toward thinking about the iPhone and more broadly, about Apple. After much analysis, I think that there are a number of factors about the iPhone that may negate Apple's performance indicators and bring an end to all this craziness. The price tag, the learning curve with a novel interface, partnership with an exclusive carrier while many consumers are locked to an existing service provider and the lack of 3G capability (at least in the first version), just to name a few. And not to forget the consumer expectation that may turn into “over”-expectation no matter how good the product is, thanks to all the media frenzy and hype. Have we become binary artists?

Regardless of what moves us to think of a new gadget as 0 or 1, the point I wish to make is that there is a lot more involved than just the iPhone. This is not about providing a new dining option for gadget-hungry consumers; it is about the information revolution. It is about the evolution of society that is adapting to the ever-increasing presence of and dependence on information in every facet of our lives. For Apple, more importantly, this is about gradually creeping into our lives and hooking up with multiple human senses in ways that we may not even fully imagine! Whether or not the iPhone lives up to its expectations, it is one of many steps in the information trail, which has only begun to crawl recently. Once again, let's think about the Apple progression in recent years - the iPod and its clickwheel interface, the Mac Mini with FrontRow for a media center interface, the AirPort Extreme for wireless networking, the AppleTV for broadcasting iTunes content to a TV and now, the iPhone as an iPod, mobile phone and Internet device in the palm of our hands.

In conclusion, I think that there is a very gradual progression of information and technology that involves us and is taking place around us. Each of the products mentioned above (and many more from Apple and other players) are building the bridge between us humans, our society and information that defines our very lives. I urge you to re-think the notion that the iPhone is the reason for Apple's success and every other positive connotation with the Apple brand name. In the short term, yes; however, in the long term, the iPhone by itself is not the sole product or service to do it. For that matter, it will be the sum total of all the innovations centered around information that will distinguish the so-called winners from the also-rans. If I were to guess, the winners will likely comprise a mash-up of players who will facilitate exchange on board a common platform, creating a balance between innovation, openness and solidifying human-computer interaction. As a final thought, review the form factor of the Mac Mini, the AirPort Extreme and the AppleTV. Now think of these stacked neatly into a media closet in your living room - do you begin to see how it's not just the iPhone?
